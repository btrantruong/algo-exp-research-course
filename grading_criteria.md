# Grading Criteria

Draft. Weights and milestones are proposals.

## General rules
All deliverables should be submitted on time. Analysis is reproducible (scripts and data in a shared repo).

## Required Coursework

Unless mentioned otherwise, deliverables are per group. Individual grades are accounted for only through the peer-graded work ethic component.

| # | Deliverable | Sem | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Project proposal: research question, 3–5 key papers, hypothesis | 1 | 1–2 pages |
| 2 | Design doc / pre-registration: manipulation, measures, sample, analysis plan | 1 | 3–4 pages |
| 3 | Pilot and materials check | 1 | Short memo |
| 4 | **Intro + Methods draft** (end of Sem 1) | 1 | ~2,500 words |
| 5 | Midterm presentation: design pitch and feedback | 1 | 10 min + Q&A |
| 6 | Data collection and analysis | 2 | Code and data repo |
| 7 | **Full research report** | 2 | ~5,000 words |
| 8 | Final presentation | 2 | 15 min + Q&A |
| 9 | Peer evaluations (individual) | 1 and 2 | Answer on provided form |

## Research Report (50%)

- This part is graded by mentors. Each criterion is scored 0–4. The total grade is 40% for the Sem 1 draft (Intro + Methods), 60% for the full paper in Sem 2.

- General rule-of-thumb for paper: One coherent story from question to conclusion. Claims no stronger than the evidence. Pre-registered and exploratory analyses kept separate.

- **Sem 1 checkpoint:** A "Fully developed" Intro and Methods section means pilot-ready, with no open design decisions.


| Section | Weight | What mentors look for |
|---|---|---|
| **Introduction** | 20% | Motivation tied to algorithmic curation. Literature synthesized, not listed. Gap stated. Focused research question. Specific, falsifiable hypotheses that follow from the literature. |
| **Methods** | 30% | Design matches the question. Intervention specified well enough to replicate (intervention depth). Valid measures beyond one generic item (measurement depth). Sample, power rationale, and recruitment reported. Analysis plan stated in advance. Ethics and consent addressed. Feasibility shown, e.g. through the pilot. |
| **Results** | 20% | Analyses match the plan; deviations flagged. Correct and complete statistics (effect sizes, CIs). Clear figures and tables. Exploratory findings labeled. Honest reporting, including null results. |
| **Discussion** | 20% | Findings interpreted against hypotheses and prior work. Explainability: how or why the effect occurred. Specific limitations (validity threats, sample, ecological realism). Implications and next steps follow from the data. |
| **Writing, references, reproducibility** | 10% | Clear, concise prose. Consistent, checked citations. Documented code and data. Legible figures. |

## Presentation (25%)

Graded by mentors (70%) and peers (30%).

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Content: clear question, justified design, correct interpretation | 40% |
| Structure and clarity: logical story, readable slides, on time | 30% |
| Delivery: all members present, confident, not read from slides | 15% |
| Q&A: accurate answers, limitations acknowledged | 15% |

## Work Ethic (25%)

Graded by peers via anonymous evaluations at the end of each semester. Criteria:
- Reliability: attends meetings, meets deadlines
- Quality of contribution
- Communication and collaboration
- Initiative and problem-solving

Mentors may adjust scores if evaluations look biased or retaliatory.

## Open Decisions

- [ ] **Individual vs. group grade.** Report is one grade per group. Add an individual adjustment, or handle only through work ethic?
- [ ] **Semester split.** Are we grading both semester together? 
- [ ] **Pre-registration.** Required or optional?
- [ ] **AI tool policy.** What is allowed for writing, coding, literature search? Require disclosure?
- [ ] **Ethics approval.** Is IRB / ethics review required before data collection? If so, set a deadline.
